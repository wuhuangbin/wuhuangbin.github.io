About Me

My research interests lie in political institutions, bureaucratic behavior, and public policy implementation, with a particular focus on China. I am interested in how institutional incentives and political contexts shape the behavior of public officials and the implementation of public policies.

My current research examines how political expression operates within bureaucratic institutions. One ongoing project studies whether unexplained variation in the tone of local government work reports, relative to the tone expected given observable local conditions, is associated with officials’ career advancement, and how this relationship varies across institutional contexts.

More broadly, I seek to understand how political institutions shape bureaucratic decision-making, how public officials respond to institutional incentives, and how these processes influence governance and policy outcomes. My research uses quantitative methods, text analysis, and survey-based approaches.
